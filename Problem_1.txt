Observations:
  - NLP vs Simple tokenizer differences:

  NLP tokenizer (WordNet lemmatization) reduces inflected forms to their root — "armies"→"army", "running"→"run" — so related forms share the same vocabulary slot, producing a denser, semantically cleaner term-document matrix. Simple tokenizer treats "army" and "armies" as distinct terms, splitting signal across variants. The trade-off is speed: NLP takes ~3× longer for the tokenization phase (709s vs 242s at numFreq=5000, k=25), though the SVD cost at high k dominates both.

  - Quality of latent concepts:

  Query run with numFreq=5000, k=25

  Quality is poor. Of the 25 concepts, roughly 15 are variations of the same theme: calendar and date articles (months 3–18 are almost all "September 1", "February 14", "July 20"… pages). Concept 4 is decade articles, concept 20 is US county disambiguation pages, concepts 23–24 are telecom stub articles. Only concepts 21 (Greek mythology) and 22 (surnames) show genuine topical coherence.

 The root cause is the corpus composition — the 41,784-article Wikipedia dump is dominated by stub/disambiguation/calendar pages which form a tight cluster that absorbs most singular value mass, leaving only residual signal for real topics. Search results confirm this: "computer science" returns telecom stubs, "history ancient empire" returns Greek mythology stubs, "football soccer player" returns disease lists. Increasing k to 100+ would give the algorithm room to separate real topics from the calendar-page noise.
